How to use

Tap the grid to drop the selected marker — or drag a marker to move it. Placement is free: drop it anywhere inside a subkeypad, not just its centre, and the range is worked out from that exact spot. Tapping a marker on the grid selects it. The dashed ring is the 1,500 m max range, the small ring is the 80 m minimum. Zoom with the + / − buttons, the mouse wheel or a pinch; the markers stay the same size on screen as you zoom, so they never cover the keypad you're aiming at. Zoom runs to 2,000% — far enough that a single grid square fills the screen; past 300% each square shows its keypad numbers (1–9), and past 1,000% the subkeypad numbers too. When zoomed in, drag empty ground to pan (a plain tap still places the marker), and Reset zoom returns to the full map. Add Mortar places a second mortar (green) with its OWN target mark (TARGET 2, blue) — each mortar aims at its own target: the panel shows the selected mortar's solution to its own target, with both solutions listed underneath. Lock mortar pins a marker in place: while it's locked, tapping or dragging on the grid won't move it. Every marker carries its own padlock on the grid — an open padlock means unlocked, a filled one means locked, and tapping the padlock (or the Lock buttons above) toggles it. Typed refs below still move a locked marker, since those are deliberate. Keypad numbers shows or hides just the keypad and subkeypad numbers that appear when zoomed in. The main grid refs inside each square (like G10, L9) and all the grid lines always stay. The in-game sight dial is the barrel-elevation dial turned on its side like the in-game gauge — the half-circle arc stands upright with its apex pointing left, and only a 300-mil window of the scale is shown at a time, centred on your barrel angle under a fixed needle pointing left; as the range moves, the window slides top to bottom with it (near 1,550 mils it shows 1,400 at the top to 1,700 at the bottom, not the whole scale) — plus the exact range and angle to set in the game. The compass strip lives only in the Mini Game, at the bottom of its training dial — the Firing solution panel has no compass. It works like the in-game compass tape: each mortar icon carries an arrow showing which way that mortar is turned, and while the Mini Game is open you turn it with the A / D keys or the A / D buttons under the game's strip (A left, D right, hold to keep turning, Shift for ±10°). The strip's degree numbers and cardinal marks slide as you turn; a solid triangle rides the strip at the target line's bearing — turn until the solid triangle lines up under the small hollow triangle fixed at the top centre, and the mortar's arrow then points straight down the target line (the arrow and both triangles turn green when you're lined up). Each mortar keeps its own facing, like its own dial. Mini Game is a training minigame: a random target is marked on the map with an orange diamond — an infantry target instead appears as small dots like the squads defending a FOB / Hideout / Cache — 1–5 squads of a random 1–8 men each, scattered over real ground — squads hold separate spots up to about 55 m apart, so the dots spread wider as you zoom — and re-rolled every round, not one tight cluster — and a vehicle target instead gets 1–3 top-down vehicle icons (truck silhouettes in the orange target marking, spread around the mark, re-rolled every round) — and you get its range plus a range calculator inside the game panel — type a range and it gives you the angle, like the in-game calculator — then dial that angle in on the game's own dial with the W / S keys (W raises the barrel, S lowers it) or the on-screen W / S buttons, then FIRE — the game's dial shows no mils number for the setting you have dialled: like in game you read it off the sight scale (numbered every 100 mils, ticks every 5), and finer than the 5-mil ticks is pure guessing: the shot is animated on the map — the shell in flight with a dashed trail in the air following it (the dashes grow behind the shell, nothing is drawn ahead of it), and a burst with an impact mark where the round lands — and rounds scatter like in game, so even a perfect dial isn't always a direct hit; hits are judged on where the round actually falls against that target's own hit rings — every type has different, small direct / close / blast radii sized to the target itself (a FOB is only a small tent and a Cache only a weapon box, so both have tight rings) — but the rings are NOT drawn and their metres are never shown: like in game there is no visible hit radius, so you read the fall of shot and adjust. The orange diamond is only a mark, and a mark is not always accurate — how far it can sit from the real target varies with who called it in: tightest when a friendly spotter is lasing it with laser designator binoculars (an accurate mark — his green FRIENDLY SPOTTER icon stands 50–150 m from the target on the grid, in real ground metres so the gap depends on the map — and Ask spotter then puts the lased position on the grid map as a square with a centre dot — only temporarily, for a few seconds), tight from a sniper team's spotter (90–200 m back, same green spotter icon) and when the commander is watching from the sky on the UAV, looser from a regular infantryman with binoculars (40–110 m out, the SAME green friendly spotter icon, labelled REGULAR INFANTRY SPOTTER) or a squad marking on the ground, and roughest from a squad that just stumbled onto the target while passing — and any ground spotter can be spotted by the enemy as the round goes on and then be killed where he sits or run away from the target to survive, after which he gives no corrections for the rest of the round; the status card names the source each round, never the error, and the defenders, vehicles and emplacements sit at their true spots, so a rough mark can sit visibly off its position. Each game target has a type: moving Infantry runs on a timeout — it keeps moving and escapes when it runs out, with a long 60 seconds and a slow on-foot pace — vehicles are always stationary, parked where they sit, so you can set up on them without chasing a moving target — and once a mortar round lands on the moving infantry (a man down, or a burst in its blast) its dots stop moving for the rest of the round, gone to ground where they stand — a Smoke mission is a mission, not a target: an infantry squad asks for smoke on a marked point so it can advance behind the screen, the point stays fixed, and its clock is varied — sometimes the squad is on a timed advance (a varied number of seconds, with the friendly squad shown as green dots moving toward the smoke point as the clock runs down, reaching it when the clock empties), and sometimes there is no timeout at all and the squad simply holds and waits — a round landing inside its generous blast ring puts the screen up, and smoke missions get no spotter correction and no true-position reveal (Skip target just calls the mission off) — a Danger close mission can also come up, Long Tan style: 1–2 friendly squads (a varied 1–5 men each, green dots) are pinned down and surrounded by 2–5 separate enemy infantry squads (orange dots) closing in on them from every side, asking for mortar support on their own position — each friendly squad starts at 100 HP and bleeds 0.02 HP per second for each enemy man still up (just 0.06 HP/s if only 3 men are pressing, 0.16 HP/s against a full 8-man squad, 0.32 HP/s with 16 men up), so every enemy man you kill slows the bleeding — an enemy squad hit by a mortar burst also stops closing in and goes to ground where it stands (the squads you haven't hit keep pressing) — and your own rounds landing close to a friendly squad wound it too, and the mission is won when every enemy man is down and lost the moment a friendly squad reaches 0 HP — while a Cache, FOB (conventional forces — BLUFOR / Russia / MEC / China always build a FOB) or Hideout (insurgency only — it replaces the FOB) has health (50 / 25 / 10 HP for a direct / close / shrapnel hit inside its rings) and takes several rounds to destroy — but scattered targets carry NO shared health pool, because their men and vehicles stand tens of metres apart: Infantry (moving, or holding a flag or building — stationary, it never moves or escapes) goes down man by man, each man only when a round lands close to HIM where his squad is standing, and a stationary Vehicle target's 1–3 vehicles each have their OWN HP pool, damaged only by rounds landing close to that vehicle — a round bursting in the empty middle of a scattered target hurts nobody; there is no standalone Emplacement target — a FOB or Hideout usually has varied emplacements dug in near it, up to 5 and never more — but sometimes (about 1 round in 4) it has none at all, a bare position with no emplacements around it, re-rolled every round from AT TOW, AA MANPAD, MG Nest and Mortar pit, with up to two Mortar pits at once (the 5-max roll is TOW + AA MANPAD + MG Nest + 2× Mortar; TOW, AA MANPAD and 2 Mortars can all sit near the same FOB / Hideout together), each with its own HP — the FOB / Hideout / Cache itself is the PRIMARY target: destroying it is target down and ends the mission even if everything around it is still up, while every emplacement, defender or parked vehicle destroyed around it is only a BONUS — each one counts +1 targets down in the score, but none of them, and not even all of them together, ends the mission while the structure stands; a FOB, Hideout or Cache is also surrounded by defending infantry — a random 1–2 squads of a random 1–8 men each, at random positions around the position (not on the structure itself), re-rolled every round — and a FOB, Cache or Hideout also gets a random 1–2 trucks and 1–2 cars / jeeps parked in a group on a random side, re-rolled every round too, so every round's surroundings look different; shells take down the men and vehicles they land close to, and each of those is a bonus target down in the score — but the mission itself is done only once the structure is destroyed, even with emplacements, defenders and parked vehicles still up around it; each mortar holds 5 rounds of each shell type (HE and Smoke) per reload; RELOAD is separate from each mortar — RELOAD MORTAR 1 and RELOAD MORTAR 2 each load only their own mortar's shells 2 at a time on their own timer (both can reload at once; R reloads the selected mortar), each pair taking 5–10 seconds, so a full reload from empty takes three batches — but you don't always have to wait for the full 5: while a mortar is reloading, its own Skip batch button stops only that mortar's reload at the shells already loaded (2 after one batch, 4 after two) so it can fire straight away with a partial magazine, and its RELOAD again tops it up later. If a game round just won't land, Skip target in the game panel gives the round up and reveals the target's true position with a green TRUE POSITION marker, its true grid ref, range and the angle that would have hit. After any round lands, Ask spotter for correction radios whoever is spotting the target — the commander on the UAV or a dedicated ground spotter (laser, sniper team or regular infantry) — and they call back a correction for the selected / active mortar only. Ask spotter is disabled when the mark came from a Passing Squad or a Squad Mark — neither is watching the fall of shot, so on those rounds the button stays dead and you walk the rounds in yourself (danger close is the exception: the pinned squad calls one coordinate to shoot — nearest squad when they face 2, largest when they face 3 or more — and Ask spotter there only says whether a round landed in the right place or not), worked out from that mortar's own last landed round (each mortar's fall of shot is kept separately — Mortar 2's correction is never handed to Mortar 1): which way its last round fell, in metres measured from the target both ways — long/short and left/right — and a relative correction in metres from the mark — “add 40 m from the mark” when the round fell short, “drop 40 m from the mark” when it fell long — plus the left/right correction in metres from the target (turn that mortar's arrow the other way — fire follows the arrow, not the mark) — never an exact range and never exact dial mils, the spotter speaks only in metres; the metres are tightest from the UAV and a laser designator — and on a laser round Ask spotter also draws the lased position on the grid, while the other spotting sources still give their fall-of-shot info after every shell dropped. A Passing Squad or Squad Mark gives no Ask spotter correction at all — but on those rounds a Request check on mark button appears under Ask spotter: press it and a checker — a commander UAV flying in, an infantry squad walking in on foot, or a vehicle driving in, rolled fresh every request — comes from far away across the grid toward the mark — but a ground checker never walks or drives right up onto the target: the infantry stops about 120 m short and the vehicle about 180 m short, and they check through binoculars from there; only the UAV flies over the mark — and from its stop point it reports the checked target position: how far the mark sits off and which way the true target lies from it, with an amber CHECKED POSITION diamond on the grid for a few seconds (the UAV checks tightest, to the nearest 5 m and fastest; the vehicle to 10 m; the infantry on foot slowest, to 15 m). One check per round. A commander-called mark also gets the commander's UAV overhead: a spinning dashed ring with a drone badge around the orange diamond and a UAV FUEL bar on the game status card, like the commander screen — the UAV burns 0.2% fuel a second travelling in and 0.33% a second on station (it refuels at base at the same 0.33% rate), when it runs low on fuel the ring turns orange but the UAV can still see from the sky, so Ask spotter keeps working on low fuel — only when it RTBs at 0% does the ring vanish and Ask spotter die for the rest of that round; targets called in by a dedicated ground spotter keep their spotter unless the enemy spots him (killed, or fled away from the target) — but a Passing Squad or Squad Mark has no spotter at all, so Ask spotter stays disabled on those rounds (danger close excepted — the pinned squad only confirms right place or not). If a second mortar is placed, FIRE is separate from each mortar: FIRE MORTAR 1 fires only Mortar 1 and FIRE MORTAR 2 fires only Mortar 2 — each firing where its OWN arrow faces, on its OWN dial angle, spending one round from its OWN magazine. Both mortars do NOT use the same dial angle: pick a mortar with the MORTAR 1 / MORTAR 2 DIAL tabs above the dial, dial that mortar's angle for its own target's range with W / S, and it stays stored for that mortar when you switch to the other one. In the Mini Game the HE / Smoke switch picks the shell type, every mortar has its own ammo for each shell type (5 HE and 5 Smoke per mortar), and each mortar's own RELOAD button refills only that mortar's magazine for the selected shell. In the game your mortars fire where their arrows face — not at the target mark and not straight at the orange game target — so drop each mark on the orange diamond (the red TARGET mark for Mortar 1, the blue TARGET 2 mark for Mortar 2), then turn each mortar's arrow onto its target line with A / D until the compass strip lines up, or that mortar's rounds go wherever its arrow happens to point. The red TARGET mark moves to the centre of the grid (G7-5-5) every time the Mini Game opens — and the view pans and zooms so the red mark sits at the centre of the visible grid area on screen, in the map's own column beside the Mini Game panel and above the Controls panel, clear of every panel, with the mortar still in view; closing the game puts the view back where it was, unless you panned or zoomed while playing. The game's status card — the range to the red mark, the target type and its health / emplacements / defenders / vehicles bars — sits on its own docked at the top left of the grid while the Mini Game is open, separate from the game panel, so you can read it without scrolling the panel up and down — the card itself stays visible on open, but its Summary starts hidden by default so the prose never covers the grid on open: tap Show summary on the card to read it. The target Summary — the rundown on the target, its mark source and its surroundings — is back on the status card under the target type while the game is open — and the card is hideable so it stops covering the grid: the Summary starts hidden by default and Show summary / Hide summary on the card folds just the Summary (range and bars stay), Hide hides the whole card and leaves a small Show Target Information pill at the top left of the grid to bring it back — and the same Summary, the aim notes and the How it works rules also live in their own separate floating panel — it is not docked, so it never overlaps the Controls panel: open it with the Summary & How it works button at the bottom of the Mini Game panel when you want the aim notes or the full rules; it opens centred on the grid, you can drag it by its header, and its Close ✕ button hides it completely until you open it again. While the Mini Game is open the layout splits in two: the map grid gets its own column on the left and the Mini Game panel sits in its own column on the right, so it never covers the grid, and it does not move, and the Map selector and Firing solution panels are hidden while the Mini Game is open — they show again as soon as you close the Mini Game. The Controls panel is docked at the bottom left of the map and does not move — use its – button to minimize it down to just its header (the button becomes + to open it again); its zoom + / − buttons stay inside it, and Reset layout in its header reopens it if it was minimized and restores the other panels.

⋮⋮ Mini Game — Summary & How it works

Summary — this target

—

Aim

Put the red TARGET mark on the orange diamond — or on the scattered small dots for an infantry target (1–5 squads of 1–8 men scattered over real ground — squads can sit tens of metres apart — drawn like the squads defending a FOB / Hideout / Cache), or on the vehicle icons for a vehicle target (1–3 of them, spread around the mark) — scattered targets have no shared health: each man goes down only when a round lands close to him, and each vehicle is hit on its own (a stationary vehicle keeps its own HP), so aim at a squad or a vehicle itself, not the middle of them — or on the smoke cloud when an infantry squad asks for a smoke screen to advance behind — a smoke clock is varied (sometimes there is no timeout at all), and on a timed smoke mission the friendly squad shows as green dots moving toward the smoke cloud as the clock runs down — or, on a danger-close mission, on the orange enemy squads closing in around the green friendly squads pinned in the centre: each friendly squad bleeds health (100 HP, −0.02 HP per second per enemy man still up — only −0.06 HP/s if just 3 men are pressing) until every enemy man is down, an enemy squad hit by a round stops closing in where it stands, and your own rounds landing close wound the friendlies too — Mortar 2 fires at its own blue TARGET 2 mark — put that on the target too. Dial each mortar's range in on its own dial (the MORTAR 1 / MORTAR 2 DIAL tabs — each mortar keeps its own angle for its own target, they do not share one), then fire it with its own FIRE button. Under the dial sits the compass strip — the only compass line-up on the page (the Firing solution panel has none): turn the mortar selected on the DIAL tabs with A / D or the strip's own A / D buttons until the solid triangle lines up under the small hollow triangle fixed at the top centre, and that mortar's icon arrow points straight down its own target line — fire follows the arrow, so a mortar that isn't lined up throws its rounds off to the side — each mortar keeps its own facing, like its own dial (FIRE MORTAR 1 / FIRE MORTAR 2) for the selected shell (HE or Smoke — the switch sits above FIRE; every mortar has its own HE and Smoke magazines). Reload is separate too — RELOAD MORTAR 1 / RELOAD MORTAR 2 each refill only their own mortar, on their own timer, with their own Skip batch button. And Ask spotter corrects the selected mortar from its own last landed round — no exact range or dial mils, just add/drop metres from the mark plus a left/right correction in metres measured from the target — switch the DIAL tabs to correct the other mortar. Ask spotter is disabled when the mark is from a Passing Squad or a Squad Mark — no spotter is watching on those rounds (danger close excepted: there the pinned squad only confirms right place or not, and calls one coordinate to shoot, picked by how many enemy squads they face — 2 → nearest, 3 or more → largest). On those rounds use Request check on mark instead: a varied checker — commander UAV, an infantry squad on foot or a vehicle driving in, rolled fresh — comes in from far away and stops short of the mark — the infantry and the vehicle check through binoculars from a standoff, only the UAV goes over the mark — and reports the checked target position (amber CHECKED POSITION diamond on the grid, briefly), once per round. The spotter varies every round — laser designator, sniper team, commander UAV, regular infantry, squad or passing squad. Sometimes the mark is from a friendly spotter with laser designator binoculars (his green FRIENDLY SPOTTER icon stands on the grid 50–150 m from the target, never on the mark — the distance is real ground metres, so how far it looks depends on the map) — then Ask spotter also puts the lased accurate position on the grid map (cyan square with a centre dot, LASED POSITION) — only temporarily, for a few seconds, so put your mark on it straight away or Ask again to re-lase. A sniper team spotter (90–200 m back) and a regular infantry spotter with ordinary binoculars (40–110 m out) stand on the grid under the same green friendly spotter icon — only their labels (SNIPER SPOTTER / REGULAR INFANTRY SPOTTER) differ; neither can lase, so there is no lased mark, but they still give info on every shell dropped — the fall of shot after each round lands — and Ask spotter still corrects in metres. And any ground spotter can be spotted by the enemy as the round goes on: he is then either killed (red SPOTTER DOWN icon where he fell) or runs away from the target to survive (orange SPOTTER FLED icon where he bolted to) — either way he gives no more corrections that round. The commander UAV is never spotted that way, and even on low fuel it can still see from the sky — it only stops correcting when it RTBs at 0%. The range in the card docked at the top left of the grid is measured to the red mark, in steps of 10 so it always ends in 0 — and fire goes where the mortar's arrow faces, not to the mark or the diamond / dots: line the arrow up on the compass strip before you fire. Stuck? Skip target gives up the round and reveals the target's true position.

How it works — hidden rings & mark accuracy, emplacements (max 5, varied — sometimes none), timeout, health, defenders, vehicles, separate RELOAD / FIRE / dials / spotter per mortar, dashed shell trail, UAV ring & fuel (low fuel still corrects, RTB ends it), spotters varied every round (laser / sniper / regular infantry — same spotter icon — / UAV / squad / passing) and a ground spotter can be spotted by the enemy, killed or fled, and stop correcting
Hit rings — hidden, and the mark is only a mark: there is no single hit distance. Each target has its own small rings — direct / close / blast — sized to the target itself, but they are never drawn and their metres are never shown: like in game there is no visible hit radius, so you read the fall of shot (long/short, left/right, measured against the real target) and adjust. A FOB is only a small tent and a Cache is only a weapon box, so both have tight rings; a squad or vehicle covers a little more ground. Inside the blast, hard targets take 50 / 25 / 10 HP (direct / close / shrapnel); outside it, a clean miss. The orange diamond is just the mark that was called in, and a mark is not always accurate — how far it can sit from the real target varies with who found the target: a squad leader lasing with laser designator binoculars gives an accurate mark (only a few metres off), a sniper team's spotter on a scope and the commander watching from the sky on the UAV give tight marks, a regular infantryman with binoculars and a squad marking from the ground looser ones, and a squad that just stumbled onto it while passing (found by accident) the roughest — but never more than 50 m off: the true position is always within 50 m of the mark, whatever the source. The status card names the source each round — never the error. The defenders, parked vehicles and emplacements sit at their true spots, so a rough mark can sit visibly off the position it refers to.
FOB vs Hideout — dynamic by faction: like in game, the forward base depends on who built it. Conventional forces — BLUFOR and REDFOR alike (Russia / MEC / China) — always get a FOB (150 HP). Insurgent forces never build a FOB — they get a Hideout instead (100 HP, the insurgency equivalent). Either can come up on a round; the label on the status card and the orange diamond tells you which one it is.
Emplacements — varied, max 5, usually at the FOB / Hideout, sometimes none, never a target on their own: there is no standalone emplacement round. A FOB or Hideout usually has emplacements dug in near it — up to 5, never more — but sometimes it has none at all (about 1 round in 4 rolls a bare position: no TOW, no MANPAD, no MG Nest, no Mortar pit — just the structure, its defenders and its parked vehicles, and the Summary says “No emplacements around it this round”), each drawn as a small orange diamond with its label, spread 20–38 m around the position at separated bearings. The loadout is varied and re-rolled every round (2–5 strong) from the four built in game: AT TOW (75 HP), AA MANPAD (50 HP — the lightest), MG Nest (75 HP) and Mortar pit (100 HP — tube and stacked ammo), with up to two Mortar pits at once. So one round might be TOW + Mortar, the next AA + MG + 2× Mortar, and the fullest roll is the 5-max: TOW + AA MANPAD + MG Nest + 2× Mortar pits — TOW, AA MANPAD and 2 Mortars can all sit near the same FOB / Hideout together. Each emplacement has its own HP and hidden rings and takes 50 / 25 / 10 HP per hit band. The FOB / Hideout itself is the primary target: destroy it and the mission is done even if emplacements are still up. Emplacements are bonus only — each one destroyed counts +1 targets down in the score, but destroying all of them (or everything else around the position) does not end the mission while the structure stands — the status card tracks them all on its Emplacements row (combined bar, each variant listed, the two Mortar pits listed separately). A Cache gets no emplacements.
Defending infantry — around built positions: a FOB, Hideout or Cache is never left alone — it is surrounded by 1–2 defending squads, shown as the small orange men around the position (the squads are around the position, not on the structure itself). 1 squad is 1–8 men, so a position can have anywhere from 1 to 16 defenders, placed in squad groups around it (the status card shows the squads and how many men are still up). A shell landing within 14 m of a man takes that man down, wherever it lands — a round that misses the structure can still clear defenders, and a direct hit on the structure may leave the outer men alive. The structure is the primary target — only destroying it (0 HP) ends the mission, even if defenders are still up. Defenders are bonus only: each man down counts +1 targets down in the score, but clearing every defender (or everything else around the position) does not end the mission while the FOB / Cache / Hideout itself still stands.
Parked vehicles — FOB / Cache / Hideout only, re-rolled every round: those three also have vehicles parked around them in one group on a random side, 20–30 m out — 1–2 trucks and 1–2 cars / jeeps, in a random order and at a random bearing each round, just like the squads are re-rolled (random 1–2 squads of a random 1–8 men, at random positions) so every round's surroundings look different. A shell within 13 m of a truck or 11 m of a car / jeep destroys it — the parked group is tight enough that one well-placed round can take more than one. The structure is the primary target — only destroying it ends the mission, even with defenders and parked vehicles still up. Parked vehicles are bonus only: each truck or car / jeep destroyed counts +1 targets down in the score, but wiping them all out (or everything else around the position) does not end the mission while the structure stands; the status card tracks vehicles separately.
Aim: the Range at the top is mortar → red TARGET mark, rounded to the nearest 10 like the in-game range steps, so its last digit is always 0 — moving the red mark changes it, and it is not measured to the hidden orange diamond. Rounds fly where the firing mortar's arrow faces and scatter a little, so line the arrow up on the target line first (compass strip under the dial, A / D) — a perfect dial on an unlined arrow still misses sideways, and a perfect line-up isn't always a direct hit. Read the fall of shot (long/short, left/right) and adjust. Your dial stays put between targets.
Timeout vs health — scattered targets are per man / per vehicle: moving Infantry escapes when its timer empties — it gets a long 60 s and moves slowly on foot. Vehicles never move — every vehicle target is stationary. Once a mortar round lands on the moving infantry — a man down, or any man caught inside the burst's blast — its dots stop moving for the rest of the round: the squads go to ground where they stand (the clock still runs, and the status card's Infantry row reads “stopped — hit by mortar”). Scattered targets have no shared health pool, because their men and vehicles stand tens of metres apart: infantry (moving, or holding a flag or building — stationary, never moving or escaping) go down man by man, each man only when a round lands close to him where his squad stands, and the target is down only when every man is down — the card counts men up, not HP. A vehicle target's 1–3 stationary vehicles are each judged on their own: each has its own HP pool (50 / 25 / 10 per hit band measured to that vehicle, listed per vehicle on the card) — a round on one vehicle does nothing to the others, and a round in the empty middle does nothing at all. Cache, FOB, Hideout and the emplacements dug in near a FOB / Hideout keep their own HP and no timer as before. A smoke mission is neither: an infantry squad asks for smoke on a marked point so it can advance — the point is fixed, there is no HP and nothing to kill, and the screen is up when a round lands inside the point's generous blast ring. Its clock is varied, rolled every smoke round: sometimes the squad is on a timed advance of a varied length — then the friendly squad appears as green dots starting short of the point and moving toward the smoke icon as the clock runs down, and the mission fails if they reach it first — and sometimes there is no timeout at all: the status card's Timeout row reads “No timeout”, no green squad moves, and the squad holds until the screen is up or you call the mission off. Smoke missions get no spotter correction, and Skip target just calls the mission off — it reveals no true position.
Danger close — surrounded squad (Long Tan style): some rounds are a mission, not a target: 1–2 friendly squads (green dots in the centre), each a varied 1–5 men, are pinned down and surrounded by 2–5 separate enemy infantry squads (orange dots), each a varied 1–8 men, spread on bearings all around them — and the enemy squads keep closing in while you work, until one is hit: an enemy squad that takes a mortar burst (a man down, or a shell landing inside its blast) stops closing in and goes to ground at the distance it had reached — the squads you have not hit keep pressing in, and a stopped squad still counts toward the bleed while its men are up. The friendlies marked their own position — a squad mark — and all they give the mortars is ONE coordinate to shoot: which enemy squad it points at depends on how many enemy squads they are facing — facing 2 squads they call the nearest one, facing 3 or more they call the largest one (and the last squad standing is called when only one is left); the called coordinate is in the briefing and on the status card, and it moves / switches as the squads press in and go down. Ask spotter on this mission only tells you whether a round landed in the right place or not — right place means the burst landed on an enemy squad and clear of the friendlies — never a range, never a metre correction. Each friendly squad has its own health: 100 HP, and it bleeds 0.02 HP per second for each enemy man still up — the bleed counts men, not just squads: only 3 men pressing bleed just 0.06 HP/s (about 1,670 seconds before a squad is overrun), one full 8-man squad bleeds 0.16 HP/s (about 625 seconds), and 16 men up bleed 0.32 HP/s (about 310 seconds) — so every man you kill slows it. The card's Friendlies row tracks each squad's HP and the live bleed rate, the Enemy row counts the men and squads still pressing, and the friendly dots fade man by man as their squad bleeds. Enemy men go down per man, like scattered infantry — a shell only takes the men it lands close to, where that squad stands now. And it cuts both ways: your own round landing within 24 m of a friendly squad's centre wounds it (30 / 15 / 6 HP the closer it falls). The mission is won when every enemy man is down, and lost the moment any friendly squad reaches 0 HP — overrun by the drain, or finished by your own fire.
Magazine, separate FIRE, separate dials & separate RELOAD: each magazine holds 5. RELOAD is separate from each mortar too — RELOAD MORTAR 1 and RELOAD MORTAR 2 are different buttons: pressing one reloads only that mortar's magazine for the selected shell, on its own timer, and both mortars can be reloading at the same time (R reloads the selected mortar). Each reload loads shells 2 at a time — every 2 shells take 5–10 s, so the pips fill in pairs (the last batch is 1 shell if only 1 is needed) and a full reload from empty is 3 batches. A mortar can't fire while IT is reloading (the other mortar still can — and can start its own reload). You don't always have to top up to 5: while a mortar is reloading, its own Skip batch button stops only that mortar's reload at the shells already loaded — after one batch that's 2 shells, after two batches 4 shells — and it can FIRE straight away with that partial magazine (2–4 rounds is often enough); press its RELOAD again later to top up. FIRE pauses 0.5 s between shots. FIRE is separate from each mortar — FIRE MORTAR 1 and FIRE MORTAR 2 are different buttons: pressing one fires only that mortar, in the direction its own arrow faces, and never fires the other. Each mortar also has its OWN dial angle for its own target: the MORTAR 1 / MORTAR 2 DIAL tabs above the dial pick which mortar W / S is turning, each mortar's mils are stored separately and restored when you switch back, and each FIRE button shoots on its own mortar's stored dial — they do not share an angle, because their ranges to their own targets are different. Your current mils are never shown: like in game there is no number for the dial you have set — no readout under the dial, none on the MORTAR 1 / MORTAR 2 DIAL tabs, none in the window label. You read your angle off the sight scale itself (numbered every 100 mils, ticked every 5), and anything finer than the 5-mil ticks is pure guessing, exactly as in game. The range calculator still tells you the angle to aim for — setting the dial to it is by eye. The spotter never does: no exact range, no exact dial mils, only a relative correction in metres from the mark (add / drop metres for the range) — plus a left/right correction in metres measured from the target, applied by turning that mortar's arrow (A / D), since fire follows the facing. Each mortar has its own magazine for each shell type (HE 5 and Smoke 5 per mortar): the HE / Smoke switch above FIRE picks the shell, each mortar's own RELOAD button refills only that mortar's magazine for the selected shell, and a mortar with none of the selected shell left holds fire on its own button. In the air the shell is followed by a dashed trail that grows behind it — no solid line is drawn ahead of the shell. A Smoke shell never damages anything — it only puts a screen up where it lands — and a smoke mission needs a Smoke shell: an HE burst there puts no screen up.
Skip target — reveal the true position: if a round just won't land, Skip target gives the round up (streak reset, no score) and shows where the target actually was: a green TRUE POSITION marker appears at the real spot, with a dashed line back to the orange mark so you can see how far the mark was off, and the feedback gives the true grid ref, the true range and the angle that would have hit. Then press Next target for a fresh round.
Spotter correction — ask whoever is spotting it: after a round lands, Ask spotter for correction radios whoever is spotting the target — the commander on the UAV or a dedicated ground spotter (laser designator, sniper team or regular infantry) — and they call back a correction from the fall of shot. Ask spotter is disabled when the mark came from a Passing Squad or a Squad Mark: the passing squad has moved on and a squad mark is just a mark, so nobody corrects on those rounds — the button stays dead and you walk the rounds in from the fall of shot yourself. Danger close is the one exception: the pinned squad's mark is a squad mark, but they stay on the radio for a limited check — they call one coordinate to shoot (which squad depends on how many they face: 2 → nearest, 3 or more → largest) and Ask spotter there only tells you whether the round landed in the right place or not, never a correction: which way the last round fell, in metres measured from the target both ways — long/short and left/right (e.g. “40 m long, 30 m right of the target”) — and a relative correction in metres from the mark you already have (“add 40 m from the mark” when the round fell short, “drop 40 m from the mark” when it fell long), plus the left/right correction in metres from the target: turn that mortar's arrow the other way, by the angle that moves the fall that far at the target (a round 30 m right of the target → turn the arrow left with A) — fire follows the arrow, so moving the mark alone would not move the next round. They never give an exact range and never give exact dial mils — the spotter speaks only in metres, so you work off the mark you already have, like in game. The correction is per mortar: it corrects the selected / active mortar (the one on the MORTAR 1 / MORTAR 2 DIAL tabs, named on the button), worked out from that mortar's own last landed round — each mortar's fall of shot is kept separately, because the two mortars sit at different ranges, so Mortar 2's correction would be wrong for Mortar 1. If the selected mortar hasn't fired yet this round, its button stays dead until it has a round down. Like the mark itself, the call's precision varies with the source: a squad leader with a laser designator, a sniper team spotter and the UAV commander call it tight (nearest 5 m), and a regular infantry spotter to the nearest 15 m. A marking squad and a passing squad give no correction at all — Ask spotter is disabled on those marks. Check on mark — the Squad / Passing answer: on those rounds a Request check on mark button shows under Ask spotter instead. Press it and a checker is sent from far away to check the target position at the mark — and who comes is varied, rolled fresh on every request: the commander UAV flies in from far off and looks down on the mark from the sky (fastest, about 3 s, and the tightest check — the checked position is good to the nearest 5 m), a vehicle crew drives in from far away but stops about 180 m short of the mark and checks through binoculars from there (about 6 s, good to 10 m), or an infantry squad walks in on foot but stops about 120 m short (slowest, about 9 s, good to 15 m) — a ground checker never goes right up to the mark: the mark sits on or near an enemy target, and nobody walks or drives onto a target just to look at it, so the ground checkers check from a standoff and only the UAV goes over the mark itself. Its icon travels across the grid from 260–520 m out to its stop point while you watch, in friendly green (drone badge for the UAV, the same friendly spotter figure for the infantry, a truck silhouette for the vehicle). On arrival it reports the check: how many metres the mark sits off the true target and the bearing / compass direction of the true target from the mark, and the checked position appears on the grid as an amber CHECKED POSITION diamond with a dashed line back to the orange mark — but only temporarily, for a few seconds, so move your target mark onto it while it shows. A check needs no fall of shot (it can be requested before you fire), gives no range and no dial mils, and does not reveal or end the round — and it is once per round: sending someone across the map is a big ask. It never appears on dedicated-spotter rounds (they have Ask spotter), on a smoke mission, or on danger close (which keeps its own right-place check). Laser designator — varied every round: some rounds the mark is from a friendly spotter carrying laser designator binoculars — the status card names him and his green FRIENDLY SPOTTER icon (a soldier with binoculars raised) stands on the grid 50–150 m from the target, never on the mark, with a faint dashed line to the mark he is lasing — the distance is rolled every round in real ground metres, so how far he sits from the target on screen depends on the selected map's size — his mark is accurate, and when you Ask spotter he lases the target and the accurate lased position is shown on the grid map as a cyan square with a centre dot (LASED POSITION) with a dashed line back to the orange mark — but it only shows temporarily, for a few seconds, then it fades off the grid: put your target mark on it while it shows, or Ask spotter again to re-lase. On rounds without a laser — a sniper team or regular infantry spotter — there is no lased mark on the grid, but they still give info on every shell dropped: the fall-of-shot feedback after each round lands, plus the metre correction above when you Ask spotter. A squad mark or passing squad gives the fall-of-shot feedback after each round too, but no Ask spotter correction — the button is disabled on those marks. More spotter variety: the spotter is re-rolled every round — laser designator, sniper team, commander UAV, regular infantry with binoculars, squad mark or passing squad — and the three single-man ground spotters (laser, sniper, regular infantry) all stand on the grid under the same green friendly spotter icon (a soldier with binoculars raised); only the label beside it, his distance from the target (laser 50–150 m, sniper 90–200 m, regular infantry 40–110 m) and his precision differ, and only the laser man can lase. Spotted by the enemy: a ground spotter sits still with his binoculars up, and sometimes the enemy spots him — in a bit under half of ground-spotter rounds he is found after 2–4 landed rounds, and every Ask spotter call can give him away too. When that happens he is either killed where he sits (his icon turns red, crossed, SPOTTER DOWN, where he fell) or he runs away from the target to survive (his icon moves with him, orange, SPOTTER FLED, at the ground he bolted to, well away from the target) — and either way he cannot give corrections any more that round: the Ask spotter button dies and you walk the rounds in from the fall of shot yourself. UAV exception: a commander-called target loses its spotter only when the UAV actually RTBs at 0% fuel (next point) — even on low fuel the UAV can still see from the sky, so it keeps correcting while it is up there.
Commander UAV — ring & fuel: when the commander calls the target in, his UAV is overhead watching it: the orange mark gets a slowly spinning dashed ring with a drone badge on top of it, and the status card gets a UAV FUEL bar like the one on the commander screen. The ring is drawn at a real 30 m ground radius centred on the mark — it grows and shrinks with the zoom like real ground, and since a commander mark sits at most 15 m off, the true position always stays inside it; the ring is never re-centred on the true position. Per the manual the UAV burns fuel the whole time it is up — 0.2%/s while travelling (about 4 s flying in) and 0.33%/s while operating over the target — and it refuels at base at the same 0.33% rate, so each commander round starts on a part-refuelled tank (50–95%). The ring and bar are grey en route, green on station and orange at low fuel (20%); at 0% the UAV RTBs and the ring vanishes. Low fuel is only a warning: even on low fuel the UAV can still see from the sky, so Ask spotter keeps working while it is up there — it only dies when the tank hits 0% and the UAV RTBs, so spend his corrections before then. Targets called in from the ground are unaffected by fuel: a dedicated ground spotter (laser, sniper, regular infantry) stays available all round — unless the enemy spots him (see Spotter correction above). A Squad Mark or Passing Squad mark has no spotter to ask in the first place — Ask spotter is disabled on those rounds.
